The system map and its hard invariant

One boundary matters more than the rest: the only place where a ledger statement becomes an irreversible chain event.

Matching engine

Single-writer sequencer

A total ordered command log makes execution deterministic and replayable.

Concrete use: Sequence order 9,184,201 reserves balance, enters a BTC-USD limit order, and emits all resulting fills before 9,184,202.

Failure mode: Distributed writers create conflicting price-time order and irreproducible books.

Price-level book

Ordered price map points to FIFO queues; add/cancel is near O(1) within a known level and best-price lookup follows the ordered structure.

Concrete use: A post-only bid at $62,500 joins the tail of that price level unless it would cross.

Failure mode: Floating-point price keys create equality and ordering defects.

Order semantics

Limit, market, stop, IOC, FOK, post-only, and iceberg orders change when/how quantity enters the book.

Concrete use: A 5 BTC FOK must fill completely immediately or cancel without partial execution.

Failure mode: Implementing order labels without exact atomic behavior produces customer disputes.

Self-trade prevention

Prevent related accounts from matching themselves under an explicit cancel-new/cancel-old/decrement policy.

Concrete use: Apply the configured account-group rule before a fill enters the journal.

Failure mode: Post-trade cleanup cannot undo misleading market data.

Recovery

Snapshot + append-only journal restores state, then deterministic replay produces the identical book and sequence.

Concrete use: Load snapshot at sequence 9,180,000, replay 4,201 commands, compare state hash before reopening.

Failure mode: A database balance snapshot cannot reconstruct queue priority.

Withdrawal pipeline at scale

Reservation

Place an immutable hold before any external action so available balance cannot be spent twice.

Concrete use: A $25,000 USDC withdrawal moves available → withdrawal-hold in one serializable transaction.

Failure mode: Debiting after broadcast allows concurrent spending.

Batching

Combine compatible screened intents while retaining output-to-intent mapping.

Concrete use: Ten Bitcoin P2WPKH withdrawals share one version/locktime/input set; each output maps to one internal ID.

Failure mode: One tainted or urgent output can poison an entire batch.

Fee spike

Queue policy distinguishes urgency, customer quote, ceiling, and replacement path.

Concrete use: Hold low-priority withdrawals above 200 sat/vB while expiring quotes and showing queue state.

Failure mode: Flat fees without cost accounting socialize extreme chain cost invisibly.

Outage drain

Rate-limit release of backlog across policy, signer, chain, and support capacity.

Concrete use: Drain 1,000 queued transfers in value tiers with fresh rescreening and nonce/UTXO allocation.

Failure mode: Releasing all at once resembles an attack and exhausts hot float.

Risk, APIs & market data

Margin boundary

Cross margin shares collateral; isolated margin bounds a position's collateral and liquidation path.

Concrete use: Journal collateral, unrealized PnL, fees, funding and liquidation as explicit accounts.

Failure mode: Mixing risk-engine projections with settled ledger balances hides insolvency.

Mark price

A robust index/mark drives margin and liquidation, not the venue's last thin trade.

Concrete use: Use multiple spot sources, staleness guards and bounded deviation.

Failure mode: A manipulable last price can trigger forced liquidation.

Snapshot + delta

Clients load an order-book snapshot then apply contiguous sequence deltas.

Concrete use: On gap 81,104 → 81,107, discard local book and resnapshot.

Failure mode: Applying deltas through a gap creates a plausible but false book.

Client order ID

Unique client identity makes order create safe to retry.

Concrete use: Query cli_20260831_771 after timeout before another create.

Failure mode: Transport timeout is not evidence of rejection.

Common mistakes & anti-patterns

The architecture cannot prevent an authorized operator from overriding controls; governance, segregation, and external assurance must make that override visible and costly.

  1. Never use binary floating point for price, quantity, fee, balance, or PnL.
  2. Never let matching engine or risk engine read live chain state.
  3. Credit only after adapter-defined finality and compliance gates.
  4. Book sweeps/rebalances as asset-location changes, not liability changes.
  5. Hold/debit before signing or broadcast; release only after authoritative terminal reconciliation.
  6. Require zero unexplained balance tolerance; model in-flight explicitly.
  7. Scan HD addresses with persisted range/lookahead and alert before exhaustion.
  8. Do not publish assets-only proof as solvency.
  9. Resynchronize market data on any sequence gap.
  10. Reconcile customer liabilities + firm positions + fees against on-chain + in-flight + receivables continuously.
