Choose the service

Start with the operating constraint, not the radio in your hand. “Emergency” is not a general waiver to use uncertified equipment or reserved spectrum; any Part 95 channel may carry an actual emergency message, with priority given to it.

FRS 8–14
0.5 W

No individual license; fixed antenna. Use for a close group where minimum interference matters.

FRS 1–7, 15–22
2 W

No individual license; fixed antenna. Use for ordinary family, hiking and neighborhood simplex.

GMRS
≤50 W

$35 / 10-year individual license; family use and repeaters. Use when antenna, mobile or infrastructure matters.

MURS
2 W VHF

License by rule; five channels; external antenna allowed within height limit. Useful for farm, shop and foliage.

Amateur
EXAM

Individual license plus exam. Use for experimentation, broad bands and amateur repeaters—not Part 95 channels.

Every channel, one register

Power units matter: FRS limits are effective radiated power (ERP); GMRS main-channel limits are transmitter output power (TPO). A 50 W rule is not permission to use 50 W on every shared channel.

The rule column, decoded

Certification attaches to the transmitter, not merely the frequency. A programmable Part 90 land-mobile radio is not automatically legal in a Part 95 service just because it can tune the channel.

FRS

  • License by rule; no application.
  • Certified FRS unit only.
  • Integral, non-removable antenna; gain ≤ half-wave dipole.
  • 22 shared channels; no repeaters.
  • Personal or business voice is allowed.

GMRS

  • Individual license required: $35 as verified 2026-09-01.
  • Normally 10 years; no exam.
  • Covers named immediate-family relationships in §95.1705.
  • Certified GMRS unit; repeaters and external antennas allowed.
  • Personal or business communications allowed.

MURS

  • License by rule; certified MURS equipment.
  • 2 W transmitter output.
  • External antenna permitted; antenna top may not be more than 60 ft above ground or 20 ft above the highest point of its supporting structure.
  • No store-and-forward repeater operation.
  • Voice, data, image and telemetry permitted.

Part 90 radio

  • Part 90 certification alone does not authorize Part 95 operation.
  • A model must hold the appropriate certification for the service.
  • Frequency capability is not legal authority.
  • Do not use a software lockout as a substitute for certification.

Amateur

  • Part 97 license and examination.
  • Experiment on amateur allocations, not Part 95 channels.
  • Amateur equipment is not thereby FRS/GMRS/MURS-certified.
  • Use the linked Technician reference for entry.

Eight repeater pairs

Your radio listens on the 462 MHz output and transmits 5 MHz higher on the matching 467 MHz input. “Open” means the owner permits general use; it does not mean tone-free or unmanaged. Follow the published access terms and identify with your GMRS call sign.

Tones are squelch—not privacy

CTCSS sends a continuous sub-audible tone; DCS sends a coded low-rate pattern. A receiver with a matching setting unmutes. Everyone on the RF channel still collides, and any receiver in carrier-squelch/monitor mode can hear the audio.

Range is mostly height and obstruction

Power helps a weak link; it does not bend UHF through a ridge. Treat the values below as conservative planning bands for intelligible handheld voice, not promises. Local noise, antenna efficiency, body position, buildings and terrain can move the result outside the band.

Common mistakes

The expensive failures are category errors: confusing power with range, tone with privacy, frequency with authorization, or a repeater output with its input.

  • “Privacy code” means privacy. It only mutes unwanted audio locally; others still hear and collide with you.
  • Channel 20 is nationally reserved. It has historical travel/emergency convention, not exclusive FCC status; emergencies have priority on every channel.
  • A business can use one employee's GMRS family license. The current individual license covers specified immediate family—not a workforce.
  • Any programmable radio is legal. Part 95 equipment certification and service rules still control.
  • More watts fixes a ridge. Move, raise the antenna legally, or use an appropriately located repeater.
  • Repeater offset alone is enough. You also need the correct pair, access permission, encode tone and identification.
  • FRS accepts a better antenna. Certified FRS antennas are integral and non-removable with a gain cap.
  • FRS and GMRS audio always matches. Authorized bandwidth differs on several shared channels; wide/narrow mismatch changes audio level.
  • Emergency need erases every rule. Use the fastest lawful path available; do not build routine plans around an imagined blanket exception.
  • Marketing miles are a plan. Test the exact route, building and antenna setup before depending on it.
