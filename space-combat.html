01 · Thesis

Victory is a changed decision

Clausewitz called war "a mere continuation of policy with other means"B. The German is mit anderen Mitteln, "with", not "by": diplomacy does not stop when the shooting starts. So the unit of victory is a changed decision, and joules delivered are only one way to change it.

Three consequences organize this sheet:

  1. Energy is a poor ranking. A weapon that boils an ocean and a message that changes a vote can produce the same treaty.
  2. As a civilization masters more physics, the cheapest route to a changed decision moves up from hulls, to industry, to information, to minds.
  3. At the top, the loser may never learn there was a war.

03 · Constraints

Laws of the arena

  1. 01Stealth is nearly impossible.

    A room-temperature hull radiates about 460 W per square meter against a 3-kelvin skyA, and a working drive is brighter by many orders of magnitude.

  2. 02You fight the enemy's past.

    Light takes 1.3 seconds from Earth to the Moon, 8.3 minutes from the Sun to Earth and 4.2 hours from the Sun to NeptuneA.

  3. 03The rocket equation taxes everything.

    A chemical rocket needs a mass ratio of about 790 to change speed by 30 km/sA.

  4. 04Heat is the real armor problem.

    Dumping one gigawatt of waste heat at 1,000 kelvin takes about 18,000 square meters of radiatorA, and radiators cannot be armored.

  5. 05Every drive is a weapon.

    A 1,000-tonne ship holding 1 g on a fusion torch puts about 5 terawatts into its exhaustA.

  6. 06At high energy, offense wins.

    Nothing stops a relativistic impactor after launch, so deterrence replaces defense.

  7. 07Bits are cheaper than joules.

    An information attack travels at light speed, costs almost nothing to copy and leaves the prize intact.

04 · Substrate

What changes when nobody is aboard

The best-known realistic space combat assumes human crews. Remove them and most of the constraints move.

Accelerationa few g sustainedlimited by structure: 100 g and up

The last row is the important one. A mind that can be backed up can also be edited, so the decisive weapons against post-human powers sit in the top rows of the chart.

4Bomb-pumped beams

nuclear × hull (C2 · R1)

  • Baseline human
  • Seconds
  • Overt
  • Disarm
What it is
A nuclear charge that spends itself driving a one-shot X-ray laser or a directed jet of plasma (the Casaba howitzer).
Scale
The United States ran ten known bomb-pumped X-ray laser tests between 1978 and 1988; reported efficiency was too low to be useful. Casaba was a 1960s study that grew out of Project Orion.B

It is single-use and must be pre-aimed; kill the carrier first.
Political use
Disarmament from beyond the enemy's laser range.
Fiction

5Neutral particle beam

nuclear × hull, also sensors (C2 · R1 + R3)

  • Baseline human
  • Seconds
  • Overt
  • Disarm
What it is
A near-light-speed stream of neutral atoms that deposits radiation inside the target, past its armor.
Scale
A 1 MeV, 10 milliamp neutral hydrogen beam was flown to 200 km altitude in July 1989 and workedB. That is 10 kilowatts peakA; a weapon needs roughly ten thousand times more.
Counter
Mass shielding and distance. Beam spread sets the range.
Political use
A mission kill. Dead electronics, intact hull, living prisoners.
Fiction
Staple of the genre; the real one is the better story.

10Relativistic kill vehicle

antimatter × industry; threat reaches incentives (C3 · R2 → R5)

  • AI or uploaded crew

One tonne at 0.9c carries about 28,000 megatons. Over 10 light-years it arrives about 1.1 years behind the light of its own launch.A

Annihilation only. Its existence creates first-strike pressure on both sides.

Note on antimatter
1 kg meeting 1 kg of matter yields about 43 megatonsA, but only about 40 percent comes out promptly as gamma rays and much of the rest ends up in neutrinosB. Antimatter is better fuel than warhead. Its natural military use is driving things to relativistic speed.

11Hawking black hole

gravitational × industry (C4 · R2)

  • Civilization as organism
  • Years
  • Overt
  • Coerce
What it is
A black hole of about a million tonnes, smaller than a proton, radiating its mass away.
Scale
A 606,000-tonne hole is 0.9 attometers in radius. Published power estimates range from a few petawatts to 160 petawatts depending on the modelB. Its last 1,000 tonnes evaporate as about 21 million megatons.A
Making one
The classic plan, focusing light until it collapses, was argued to be impossible in a 2024 Physical Review Letters paper because the light turns into escaping particle pairs first. A 2025 reply found idealized loopholes but agreed it is implausible in practiceB. That leaves compressing matter, or finding a primordial one.
Counter
It is the loudest gamma source in the system and cannot be hidden. It also cannot easily be fed, so it runs on a clock.
Political use
Power plant, drive and time bomb in one object. Whoever holds it holds a deadline.
Fiction
David Brin's Earth.C

What it is
A telescope parked where a star's gravity focuses light, using the star as the lens.
Scale
The Sun's focal region starts about 548 AU out. From there a 1-meter telescope could map a planet 100 light-years away at roughly 10 km resolution.B

Almost none. It is passive, tiny and far from anything. Re-aiming it by one degree means moving about 10 AU.B

06 · Common mistakes

Five myths

  1. Gravity-wave weapons.

    The black hole merger detected in 2015 turned three solar masses into gravitational waves, peaking at 3.6×1049 wattsB. Standing 1 AU away, a two-meter person would be stretched by about 0.17 micronsA. Matter is almost transparent to gravitational waves, which makes them a fine signal and a hopeless weapon.

  2. Dropping a black hole on a planet.

    A million-tonne hole radiates far more than it can swallow. Even a mountain-mass hole is about the size of a protonA, so it feeds extremely slowly. A relativistic rock is cheaper.

  3. Neutrino death rays.

    They pass through the target. The one serious proposal, a 2003 paper on fizzling nuclear warheads through the planet, needed a 1,000 TeV beam from an accelerator about 1,000 km around, which its own authors called ridiculous.B

  4. Dogfights.

    There is no air to bank against and no horizon to hide behind. A crewed fighter is a missile that carries a hostage.

  5. Fireballs and shockwaves.

    There is no blast in vacuum. A nuclear weapon in space kills with X-rays, and only at short range.

07 · Out of bounds

Beyond the line: unconfirmed physics

Grayed out on the chart. Each needs physics nobody has confirmed.

  • Faster-than-light travel or signaling
  • Dimensional strikes and unfolded-proton computers (The Three-Body Problem)
  • Strange-matter conversion
  • Magnetic monopoles catalyzing proton decay
  • Triggered vacuum decay: a doomsday device that expands at light speed and takes its user with it
  • Black holes made purely from light (argued impossible in 2024, contested in 2025)B

Who actually wins?

CoerceBlockade (8), asteroid ultimatum (9), deterrence (20)reversible, leaves someone to sign the treaty
