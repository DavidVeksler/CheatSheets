Chocolate calculation tape

Type first, then dose

Use total methylxanthines (theobromine + caffeine), not candy weight alone. Natural and brand variation means this is an estimate to communicate, not a clearance tool.

Approximate methylxanthines

Cocoa powder28.5 mg/g
Unsweetened / baker’s15.5 mg/g
85% cocoa bar*13.2 mg/g
70% cocoa bar*10.85 mg/g
65% cocoa bar*10.1 mg/g
Cocoa bean hulls9 mg/g
Semisweet / sweet dark5.3–5.6 mg/g
Milk chocolate2.3 mg/g
White chocolate0.04 mg/g

Worked case / 12 kg beagle + 100 g of 70%

1

Estimate concentration: 15.5 mg/g × 0.70 = 10.85 mg/g.

2

Total methylxanthines: 100 g × 10.85 mg/g = 1,085 mg.

3

Weight-normalized dose: 1,085 mg ÷ 12 kg = 90.4 mg/kg.

4

Compare with Merck bands: mild around 20, cardiac 40–50, seizures ≥60 mg/kg.

90.4 mg/kg → above the seizure-effect band → CALL NOW.

*Percent-cocoa estimates use Merck’s method: unsweetened chocolate concentration × labeled cocoa fraction. Filled products, cocoa butter proportions, and manufacturer variation make direct label/product identification better.

Source: Merck Veterinary Manual—Chocolate Toxicosis in Animals (updated Feb 2026).

Uncertainty is actionable

The idiosyncratic ones

“No established safe dose” does not mean “probably fine.” It means dose alone cannot rule out injury.

Grapes, raisins, tamarind

Tartaric acid is the leading toxic principle; content varies by fruit. Some dogs develop acute kidney injury while others do not. More than one grape or raisin per 4.5 kg may pose renal risk.

Use count + weight

Allium family

Cats are most susceptible. Powders and dry soup mixes concentrate onion/garlic. RBC injury is delayed: feeling normal tonight does not clear a meaningful exposure.

Keep delayed injuries in view

Macadamias

Signs have been reported at 2.4 g/kg in dogs, usually within 12 hours. The syndrome is commonly self-limiting, but chocolate coatings or xylitol add separate hazards.

Check every ingredient

Compound edible case

Chocolate + THC is not one exposure

Do not collapse a multi-ingredient edible into a single “weed” case. Calculate and report each hazard separately; sugar-free products may add xylitol.

THC signs in dogs may begin at 0.3–0.5 mg/kg, with moderate signs above 2–3 mg/kg; oral signs typically start in 1–2 hours. Source: Merck—THC toxicosis. Tell the vet the truth; accurate product and dose information changes risk assessment.

Household + garden registers

Active ingredient beats bait color

US consumer rodenticide rules changed the shelf mix. As of September 2026, EPA says consumer baits are ready-to-use stations with block/paste bait; second-generation anticoagulants are not registered for consumer products.

Anticoagulants

warfarin · chlorophacinone · diphacinone · brodifacoum · bromadiolone

Delayed coagulopathy. A normal-looking pet in the first day is not cleared.

Bromethalin

neurotoxic uncoupler

High-dose seizure syndrome or delayed paralysis. No specific antidote.

Cholecalciferol

vitamin D3 · often 0.075%

Raises calcium/phosphorus, mineralizing kidneys and other soft tissues.

Zinc phosphide

phosphine gas generator

Vomitus can release toxic gas: ventilate transport and avoid face-level exposure.

Correction strip / no home dosing

What not to do

The next correct action depends on species, airway safety, substance, formulation, time, and current signs. Do not turn an exposure into a second injury.

No peroxide for cats

There is no safe effective at-home cat emetic; peroxide can ulcerate and bleed the stomach and esophagus.

No vomiting for caustics

Drain/oven cleaners and leaking batteries can burn the esophagus again on the way back up.

No vomiting for petroleum

Gasoline, kerosene, motor oil, and similar hydrocarbons can be aspirated into the lungs.

No vomiting if symptomatic

Ataxia, depression, seizure, active vomiting, or an impaired swallow raises aspiration risk.

No vomiting with airway risk

Severe brachycephalic disease, laryngeal paralysis, megaesophagus, or sedation can prevent airway protection.

No peroxide dose from the web

Even in dogs, whether emesis is appropriate is a veterinary toxicology decision; excess peroxide injures GI tissue.

No milk, bread, oil, or grease

They do not neutralize most toxins; oils add aspiration and pancreatitis risk.

No salt as an emetic

Salt itself can cause dangerous hypernatremia, brain swelling, tremors, and seizures.

No home activated charcoal

It does not bind every toxin, is contraindicated in some exposures, and aspiration can be life-threatening.

No wait-and-see for xylitol or antifreeze

A quiet interval can consume the window before severe hypoglycemia, metabolic injury, or kidney failure is visible.

Contraindications: Merck—Principles of Toxicosis Treatment; cat peroxide and myths: Pet Poison Helpline.

Why there is no national “typical emergency-vet price” here
There is no authoritative, comparable current national tariff for US emergency veterinary visits; region, hospital, diagnostics, treatment, and hospitalization change the total. An invented range would create false reassurance. Ask the hospital for its exam fee and estimate while poison-control risk assessment proceeds; keep the case number so the treating team can continue the same toxicology case.
