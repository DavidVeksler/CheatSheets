## Why this topic

"Mercantilism vs capitalism" is the single largest row in the measured
comparison bucket: **16,847 impressions with the incumbent (mises.org) at
position 4.3 converting at only 1.28% CTR**. That CTR at that position means
searchers are seeing the result and skipping it — the ranking pages are essays,
and the searcher (almost always a student with an assignment, or someone
arguing on the internet) wants a *table*. Nobody in the SERP ships the
side-by-side grid with real dates, real policies, and real outcomes. That grid
is exactly this site's proven format.

The differentiating angle, in David-voice: **mercantilism is not a dead museum
system**. Export subsidies, tariff walls, strategic-industry protection, and
bilateral trade "wins" are mercantilist policy running today under the name
industrial policy. The sheet that says so plainly — and lets the reader classify
any real economy on concrete criteria — beats another recitation of
Adam-Smith-refuted-the-mercantilists.

## Content approach

1. **Quick Reference: the master matrix** (signature element, first screen).
   Systems as columns — Mercantilism, Free-market capitalism, Social democracy /
   welfare capitalism, State socialism (command economy), State capitalism (the
   modern China model), Corporatism/fascist economics. Criteria as rows, all
   concrete: who owns the means of production; who sets prices; trade posture;
   role of the state; what "wealth" means in the theory; monetary doctrine;
   labor's position; canonical era with dates; canonical thinkers/works;
   measured outcome of the canonical case; characteristic failure mode.
   Target: **6 systems × 11–12 criteria, every cell filled with a specific**,
   never "high/low/varies".
2. **Per-system deep cards** (one `<details>` each, ~6 entries per card):
   precise definition, the canonical historical implementation with dates and
   2–3 named policies (e.g., Navigation Acts 1651/1660; Gosplan five-year plans
   from 1928; Wirtschaftswunder reforms 1948), key thinker + key work + year,
   measured outcomes (growth rates, famine tolls, trade volumes — verified,
   dated), the standard critique, and "often confused with".
3. **Head-to-head: mercantilism vs capitalism** — the primary query answered
   under its own question-H2 in a focused two-column table plus a 3–4 sentence
   liftable answer paragraph. Include the zero-sum vs positive-sum core, specie
   accumulation vs productive capacity, and Smith's actual argument (Wealth of
   Nations, 1776, Book IV) with an archive deep link.
4. **"Mercantilism today" classification table** — modern policies (tariffs,
   export controls, sovereign wealth strategies, industrial subsidies, currency
   management) mapped to their mercantilist ancestor, with named current
   examples and dates. This is the differentiating section; keep it factual and
   dated, not editorial about any administration.
5. **Classify-a-real-economy exercise table** — 8–10 real economies (historical
   and current) scored against the matrix criteria, with a one-line verdict
   each. Every row reaches an actual classification (Rule 3: no "it's
   complicated" endings).
6. **Common mistakes** (mandatory): capitalism ≠ corporatism; socialism ≠
   welfare state (Sweden row: ~90% of firms privately owned); mercantilism ≠
   simply "old"; command economies did post growth (and how/why the numbers
   mislead); "mixed economy" is not a fourth system but a position on the grid.
7. **Related sheets + archive links** footer per cross-link map.

## Volatile-facts register

**Overall: STABLE.** Historical facts, dates, and canonical texts don't move.
Exceptions, all in sections 4–5: current tariff/industrial-policy examples
(re-verify each freshness pass against primary government sources), "2025
China/USA" classification rows (annual sanity check). Tag each such cell with
"as of <Mon YYYY>".
