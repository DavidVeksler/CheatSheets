Which of these nine sheets do I actually need?

The matrix answers that. Follow the linked decisions that your selected model leaves with you.

What does buying custody actually remove?

The signing primitive and some of its audit burden. Ledger, deposit, reconciliation, compliance-placement, and withdrawal decisions remain elsewhere in the system.

Why is “who owns this” the first question?

Unassigned decisions become incidents. Vendor documentation naturally describes what the vendor does, not the gaps around it.

I have an incident, not an architecture question.

Use the symptom router. First record what you know before changing, retrying, or re-sending anything.

Common mistakes & anti-patterns

These are routing failures. Each one leaves a decision between teams or hides it behind a product label.

  1. Choosing the vendor before the model. Name the trust boundary first, then evaluate products inside it.
  2. Reading VENDOR as “not our risk.” It identifies the operator, not who bears the outcome.
  3. Calling a hybrid a sixth model. Read both relevant columns and assign the seam explicitly.
  4. Accepting a responsibility matrix without an exit test. Contract language is not an executable export.
  5. Letting each team keep its own transaction identity. Route the break to the shared ledger intent before retrying.
  6. Using this hub as implementation guidance. Open the row's deep link; the hub deliberately does not teach the control.
