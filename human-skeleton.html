Bone shapes

ShapeDefinitionExamplesGotcha
LongCylindrical, longer than wide, with a shaft and two endsFemur, humerus, phalangesFinger and toe bones count as long bones because of their shape, not their size.
ShortRoughly as long as it is wideCarpals (8), tarsals (7)Don't confuse with sesamoid bones below; they form for a different reason.
FlatThin, often curved platesCranial bones, sternum, ribs, scapulaeSpongy bone sandwiched between two compact layers, and a major adult site of red bone marrow.
IrregularNo single characteristic shapeVertebrae, hip bones, facial bonesThe sacrum looks like one bone but is five vertebrae fused together, and fusion isn't finished until middle age.
SesamoidSmall and round, forms inside a tendonPatellaThe patella is the only sesamoid in the standard 206-bone count; others near the thumb and big toe are common but variable, so they're usually left out of the tally.

Synovial joint types

TypeMovementExampleGotcha
Ball-and-socketMultiaxial, widest range of motionShoulder, hipThe shoulder trades stability for mobility; it's the most frequently dislocated major joint.
HingeUniaxial flexion/extensionElbow, knee, finger jointsThe knee is a modified hinge: it also allows a little rotation once it's flexed.
PivotUniaxial rotation around a central axisProximal radioulnar joint, C1–C2 (atlantoaxial)The joint at C1–C2 is the one you use to shake your head "no," not the joint at the skull's base.

Common mistakes

Myth

The skull is one bone.

Fact

It's 22 separate bones (8 cranial, 14 facial) joined by immovable sutures, plus 7 more head-associated bones (the hyoid and six ear ossicles) that aren't part of the skull itself.

Myth

All 12 rib pairs attach to the sternum.

Fact

Only ribs 1–7 (true ribs) attach directly. Ribs 8–10 attach indirectly through shared cartilage, and ribs 11–12 (floating ribs) don't attach to the sternum at all.

Myth

The sacrum and coccyx have always been single bones.

Fact

The sacrum forms from five separate vertebrae and the coccyx from four. Fusion doesn't start until around age 20 and isn't finished until middle age.

Myth

The fibula carries your body weight, since it runs alongside the shin.

Fact

The tibia is the weight-bearing bone of the lower leg. The fibula bears no body weight; it's mainly a muscle-attachment and ankle-stability strut.

Myth

Sesamoid bones are a minor anatomical footnote.

Fact

The patella, the body's largest sesamoid bone, is load-bearing at the knee's extensor mechanism and is the only sesamoid counted in the standard 206-bone tally.

Myth

The cartilage in your nose, ears, and ribs counts toward the 206 bones.

Fact

Cartilage isn't bone. The nose and ear pinna stay cartilage for life, and the costal cartilage connecting ribs to the sternum never ossifies.
